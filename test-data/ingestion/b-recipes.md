# Marlowe Kitchen Notebook: Four Family Recipes

## Quillfern Dumplings
Quillfern Dumplings are a Sunday dish from the Marlowe family. The filling is 300 grams of finely chopped quillfern shoots, 150 grams of smoked tofu, two spring onions and one teaspoon of toasted fennel seed. The dough is 250 grams of flour mixed with 120 millilitres of warm water and a pinch of salt. Rest the dough for 25 minutes under a damp cloth. Steam the dumplings for 11 minutes over a bamboo rack. They are served with a dip of black vinegar and honey in a ratio of 3 to 1.

## Ambergris Lentil Stew
This stew uses 400 grams of red lentils, one diced sweet potato, two cloves of garlic and a tablespoon of smoked paprika. Fry the garlic and paprika in olive oil for 90 seconds, then add the lentils and 1.2 litres of vegetable stock. Simmer for 35 minutes, stirring every ten minutes. The secret ingredient is a spoonful of tamarind paste stirred in at the end. The stew keeps for four days in the fridge and tastes better on the second day.

## Dunmoor Oat Loaf
Dunmoor Oat Loaf is a no-knead bread. Combine 350 grams of rolled oats, 200 grams of wholemeal flour, 10 grams of salt, 5 grams of dried yeast and 400 millilitres of cold buttermilk. Let the batter rise overnight for 12 hours in a covered bowl. Bake in a cast iron pot at 220 degrees Celsius for 40 minutes, the first 25 with the lid on. Cool the loaf for one hour before slicing.

## Pemberlow Pear Tart
The tart base is 200 grams of crushed ginger biscuits mixed with 90 grams of melted butter, pressed into a 23 centimetre tin. The filling is three ripe pears, sliced thin, layered over 150 grams of almond cream. Bake at 180 degrees Celsius for 45 minutes until the edges turn deep gold. Brush with warm apricot jam while the tart is still hot. Serve with a spoonful of crème fraîche.
